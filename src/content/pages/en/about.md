<h2 id="informatics">What is architectural informatics</h2>

Architecture has always been an information practice. A building is designed, debated, built and inhabited through representations: from the plan and the parametric model to the simulation that runs alongside the model as it changes. Architectural informatics studies these representations and the computation behind them. Its concern is not how building data is managed, but how spatial information is produced, how it travels between media and between people, and how it changes what can be designed.

We pursue this question in two directions. Towards bits: learned simulation, generative models, computational analysis of form and cultural heritage, spatial media and extended reality. Towards atoms: compliant mechanisms, robotics, construction with low-tech materials, and environments that sense and respond. The questions that interest us most arise where the two meet.

<h2 id="lineage">History</h2>

The lab continues the digital architecture tradition that the Graduate Institute of Architecture at National Chiao Tung University established in the 1990s. The institute's first conference papers were presented at CAADRIA ’96; the following year it co-hosted CAADRIA ’97, and in 2026 it hosted CAADRIA again. June-Hao Hou returned to the institute to teach in 2007 after earning a Doctor of Design at Harvard University, served as its director from 2017 to 2023, and has led the lab since 2021.

<h2 id="how-we-work">How we work</h2>

Most of the lab's research is published as papers, which usually grow out of degree theses and go on to become prototypes or installations. Students have published at CAADRIA, eCAADe, SIGGRAPH Asia, HCI International and UIST; their work has been shown in museums and at new-media art festivals, and the lab's research has been funded by the National Science and Technology Council.

<h2 id="join">Join</h2>

The lab admits master's and doctoral students through the Graduate Institute of Architecture. Before writing to us, read two or three recent papers in the direction that interests you, and tell us which of their questions you would like to take further. Current members and alumni come from architecture, computer science, interaction design, mechanical engineering and the arts.
